# RSVP and the guest pass

What a guest sees from one link: the card, the reply form and their pass.

Audience: Hosts, and anyone curious about the guest side

> Archived from the former public manual. Internal reference only; content reflects the product at the time it was archived.

## One link, no app

A guest opens `/i/<token>` in any phone browser. They see the invitation card (drawn from your saved design), then the reply form. No account is needed.

## The reply form

- Accept or decline in one tap.
- If you allow it, a plus-one (with a name) and party size.
- Dietary needs, a note to the couple, and your own RSVP questions.
- After replying, the guest gets a confirmation email with their reply, date, venue and table.

## Accepted guests: the pass

Once a guest accepts, their card shows the entry seal with their own code, and they can save the invitation. This is what the usher checks at the door. The code is also available as text, so a guest never has to rely on a scan alone.

## Finding a pass later

On the public event page (`/e/[slug]`) a guest can enter their invitation code to find their pass again without the original message.

> Weight: Guest routes are held to a small budget: `/i/[token]` adds about 12 KB gzipped on top of the shared baseline, and the rule is no more than 20 KB. See [Quality and testing](testing.md).
